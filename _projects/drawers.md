# Drawers Assembly — SolidWorks to Finished Build

I modeled the full drawers assembly in SolidWorks using industry-standard components (3030 aluminum extrusions, drawer slides, fasteners and plates), then sourced the parts locally and overseas. The build documents tolerances, part selection, and lessons learned during manufacturing and assembly.

## Workflow (numbered)

1. Model the entire assembly in SolidWorks using standard component libraries and parametric features for the extrusions and plates.
2. Create a parts list (BOM) with supplier alternatives and note tolerance-critical dimensions (e.g., drawer slide alignment, plate hole positions).
3. Order extrusions, drawer slides, fasteners, and plates; account for manufacturing tolerances and finish.
4. Pre-fit and machine the top plate to final size; confirm hole alignments and countersinks.
5. Assemble the drawers, adjust slide preload, and verify smooth travel and clearances.

## Manufacturing lessons & design choices

- Account for extrusion straightness and hole placement tolerances when designing mounting plates.
- Use adjustable slot locations or oversized holes where possible to allow alignment during assembly.
- Check drawer slide alignment with a temporary jig before final fastening.
- When sourcing parts from multiple vendors, specify critical tolerances and finish requirements.

This project improved my part sourcing and manufacturing workflow, and the final drawers operate smoothly after careful alignment and finishing.

## Bill of Materials (high level)

- 3030 aluminum extrusions (cut to length)
- Drawer slides (length matched to drawer depth)
- Top plate (aluminium/steel, trimmed to final size)
- Fasteners: M5/M6 bolts, nuts, T-nuts for extrusions
- Mounting plates (laser-cut or CNC) and corner brackets
- Misc: end-caps, cable ties, threadlocker

Sources: purchased from local hardware suppliers and online vendors (example categories: extrusion suppliers, drawer slide manufacturers, fastener distributors). When ordering overseas, factor in tolerance and finish variations.

## Final notes

- The top plate was cut and test-fitted multiple times to ensure perfect alignment with the drawer slides. Trimming the plate to final size was the last mechanical step before final assembly.
- The key takeaway is to design for adjustability: slots/oversized holes and temporary jigs save time during final assembly.
